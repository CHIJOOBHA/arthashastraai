<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

You're asking about something that didn't happen. The RBI did not ban UPI fees for merchants in 2023. The zero-fee rule came from the **central government in January 2020**. In **2023**, the move went the other way: NPCI, which runs UPI, **added** a fee on one type of UPI merchant payment.

## What is claimed
That in 2023 the RBI banned all fees that merchants pay to accept UPI payments.

## What the evidence shows
**1. The ban came in 2020, from the government, through tax and payments law.**
- Fees on UPI and RuPay debit card payments were made zero from January 2020, through amendments in Section 10A of the Payments and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 and Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act, 1961 [[1]](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2114335&reg=48&lang=2).
- The tax department put it into effect with CBDT Notification No. 105/2019 under Section 269SU [[2]](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/articles/upi-zero-mdr-policy-explained-who-pays-for-free-digital-payments).
- Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced it. According to The Tribune, the stated reason was to promote digital payments [[3]](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/no-mdr-charges-applicable-on-transactions-via-rupay-upi-from-jan-1-sitharaman-18305/amp). She also said companies with a turnover of Rs 50 crore or more [[3]](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/no-mdr-charges-applicable-on-transactions-via-rupay-upi-from-jan-1-sitharaman-18305/amp) would have to accept RuPay debit cards and UPI QR codes.

**2. What actually happened in 2023 was a new fee.**
- From 1 April 2023, NPCI allowed a fee of up to 1.1% on merchant payments above ₹2,000 made from **prepaid wallets** (PPIs) through UPI. NPCI said "there are no charges for the bank account to bank account-based UPI payments (i.e. normal UPI payments)" [[4]](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/no-charge-on-normal-upi-payment-interchange-fee-for-ppi-transactions-npci-123032900477_1.html).
- NPCI said bank-account payments make up over 99.9 per cent of total UPI transactions [[4]](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/no-charge-on-normal-upi-payment-interchange-fee-for-ppi-transactions-npci-123032900477_1.html).
- The fee was 0.5-1.1% depending on merchant categories [[5]](https://payu.in/blog/upi-interchange-fees-npci-payu/). I couldn't check whether NPCI's promised review by September 2023 changed it.

**3. A possible source of the mix-up.** In December 2016, the RBI did tell banks not to charge any fee in the period for transactions charges up to Rs 1,000 [[6]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/economy-policy/rbi-slashes-mdr-charges-on-transactions-by-upto-rs-2-000-116121601193_1.html) on IMPS, USSD and UPI. That was temporary and only covered small payments.

**4. The zero-fee rule is now being partly reversed (2026).**
- In September, the Finance Ministry specified transactions of up to Rs 2,000 on UPI and RuPay-powered debit cards as protected payment modes [[7]](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-upi-mdr-rs-2000-threshold/).
- NPCI then announced a 0.4% fee on merchant payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 per transaction [[8]](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-introduces-0-4-mdr-on-upi-transactions-above-rs2000/) for purchases of ₹75,000 or more. Railways, telecom, insurance and fuel would pay a flat MDR of ₹5 per transaction [[8]](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-introduces-0-4-mdr-on-upi-transactions-above-rs2000/) on payments above ₹2,000.
- It was due to start on 15 October 2026. On 13 October, NPCI deferred the implementation [[9]](https://currentaffairs.bankexamstoday.com/2026/10/npci-defers-proposed-04-mdr-on-select.html) until immediately after Diwali. I couldn't confirm whether it has started yet.

## The other side
- **Government's case for zero fees:** push people towards digital payments and less cash. Banks weren't simply told to do the work for free. The government compensated banks, PSPs and TPAPs to the tune of 0.15% for UPI transactions below INR 2,000 [[10]](https://inc42.com/features/end-of-free-upi-why-fintech-startups-want-mdr/), but paid nothing for larger payments.
- **NPCI's case for bringing fees back:** it says running UPI costs around ₹20,000 crore annually [[8]](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-introduces-0-4-mdr-on-upi-transactions-above-rs2000/). It argues that charging only on larger payments gives more reliable funding than depending on subsidies.
- **Who it affects:** NPCI says payments up to ₹2,000 are more than 95% of total UPI P2M transaction volume [[8]](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-introduces-0-4-mdr-on-upi-transactions-above-rs2000/). Small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh per month through UPI QR [[8]](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-introduces-0-4-mdr-on-upi-transactions-above-rs2000/) directly into their accounts stay exempt.

## Verdict
**Not supported. Confidence: high.** Merchant UPI fees were banned in **January 2020** by the **government**, not by the RBI in 2023. The 2023 change *added* a fee on wallet-based payments. In 2026, the zero-fee rule is being partly rolled back.

## What you can do
- **Shoppers:** paying by UPI is still free for you. A merchant fee is charged to the merchant, not added to your bill.
- **Merchants:** compare your settlement statements with these rules. Normal bank-account UPI payments up to ₹2,000 should not have a fee deducted. If a bank or payment company wrongly charges you, complain to it first. If that doesn't work, take it to the RBI Ombudsman through cms.rbi.org.in.

**Counter-Tweet:** Myth check: the RBI didn't ban UPI merchant fees in 2023. The government did it in Jan 2020. In 2023 NPCI added up to 1.1% on some wallet payments, and a 0.4% fee above ₹2,000 is now due after Diwali 2026. Shoppers still pay nothing.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

Assuming you're in India (most of the public data on this is Indian). If you're somewhere else, tell me the country, because the rules and figures will be different.

## What is claimed

The claim is that banks "steal" through minimum balance penalties. Legally, that's not accurate. These fees are allowed: the RBI lets banks charge them under policies approved by their boards, provided they are reasonable and transparent [[1]](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/banks-earned-over-rs-7086-crore-from-minimum-balance-penalties-in-fy26). The better question is whether banks are taking far more than is fair, and doing it mostly from people who have the least. The evidence on that is strong.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The amounts are large.** In 2025-26, Indian banks collected ₹7,086.63 crore, split ₹4,948.71 crore from private banks and ₹2,137.92 crore from public sector banks [[2]](https://dailyfinancial.in/which-bank-service-charges-still-apply-in-2026-and-which-ones-quietly-disappeared/), according to the finance ministry's reply in Parliament. A parliamentary committee reported in December 2025 that "All banks collectively have amassed a staggering Rs.28,495 crore in the past five years" [[3]](https://theprint.in/economy/banks-made-rs-4818-crore-from-minimum-balance-penalties-in-fy24-25-ls-report-flags-burden-on-poor/2852966/) from these penalties.

**2. The penalty is far bigger than what you lose by not keeping the balance.** The committee found that the penalty charged can be 15 to 20 times the amount that a customer would have earned as interest for maintaining the balance [[3]](https://theprint.in/economy/banks-made-rs-4818-crore-from-minimum-balance-penalties-in-fy24-25-ls-report-flags-burden-on-poor/2852966/). An older IIT Bombay study (2017, so dated) worked out the penalties as yearly interest rates on the amount you were short. Indian Overseas Bank's charge came to 159.48 per cent a year, Yes Bank's to 112.8 per cent, followed by HDFC Bank (83.76 per cent) and Axis Bank (82.2 per cent). SBI's was 24.96 per cent. [[4]](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/banks-imposing-unreasonable-fee-for-breach-of-min-balance-117122800804_1.html)

Here's my own illustration of how that happens. It uses a sample fee table from a weakly sourced finance blog, not any specific bank's schedule. Say a bank requires ₹10,000 and charges ₹300 for a 50-75% shortfall [[5]](https://clarimoney.com/blog/minimum-balance-rules-savings-account/). You keep ₹4,000, so you're ₹6,000 short. ₹300 ÷ ₹6,000 = 5% a month, which is about 60% a year, before GST. That's like paying credit-card-level interest on money you never borrowed.

**3. Flat "slabs" punish small slip-ups.** The RBI says the penalty should be a fixed percentage levied on the amount of difference between the actual balance maintained and the minimum balance [[6]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1500). Many banks instead charge flat amounts by band, so a small shortfall can bring a big fee. Back in 2013, an RBI official said: "Sometimes, even if the minimum balance requirement falls short by Rs 100, complaints have been lodged that some banks charge over Rs 500" [[7]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rbi-cracks-whip-on-minimum-balance-penalty-110110800079_1.html).

**4. Quiet changes and forgotten accounts.** The RBI has also noted that banks often increase the minimum balance without the customer even being aware of it [[7]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rbi-cracks-whip-on-minimum-balance-penalty-110110800079_1.html). It also found that when someone stops checking an account, the bank continues to charge the account until the balance is nil [[7]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rbi-cracks-whip-on-minimum-balance-penalty-110110800079_1.html). That's why the 2015 rules now apply.

**The rules banks must follow (since April 2015):**
- You must get a notice, and banks can only charge if the balance isn't restored within a reasonable period, which shall not be less than one month from the date of notice of shortfall [[6]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1500).
- The charge must be directly proportionate to the extent of shortfall observed [[6]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1500).
- Your balance must not turn into negative balance solely on account of levy of charges for non-maintenance of minimum balance [[6]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1500).

If a bank breaks these rules, the money it took is the closest thing to what you're calling "stealing", and you can get it back.

## The other side

- **The banks' own reason:** SBI said it brought the charge back to partly cover costs for maintaining systems and operations of savings accounts [[8]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/keep-minimum-balance-in-sbi-accounts-or-pay-levy-117030300893_1.html).
- **The government's defence:** The Finance Minister said the ₹8,092.83 crore that public sector banks collected over three years was "approximately 0.23 per cent of the total income of Public Sector Banks" [[9]](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/financial-regulations/banks-made-8-092-crore-are-you-paying-minimum-balance-penalties-too/article-190487/). She said the charges were mainly about covering costs, not making revenue.
- **Zero-balance accounts are exempt:** About 730 million such zero-balance accounts remain exempt from penalties [[1]](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/banks-earned-over-rs-7086-crore-from-minimum-balance-penalties-in-fy26). These are the Jan Dhan and basic savings (BSBD) accounts.

**Testing those defences:**

## What is claimed

## What the evidence shows

**Who got the money.** SBI said 22,217 electoral bonds were purchased by donors between April 1, 2019, and February 15, 2024, out of which 22,030 bonds worth Rs.12,769.40 crore were redeemed by political parties. [[1]](http://adrindia.org/content/eci-reveals-electoral-bonds-data-bjp-tops-beneficiary-list-biggest-donor-little-known-future) The BJP cashed ₹6,060.5 cr (47.46%) [[2]](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/national/who-encashed-the-most-amount-of-electoral-bonds-in-india-bjp-tops-the-list-51204669029), then the Trinamool Congress (TMC) with ₹1,609.5 cr (12.60%), Congress with ₹1,421.8 cr (11.13%), the BRS with ₹1,214.7 cr (9.51%) and the BJD with ₹775.5 cr (6.07%). I use the 2019–24 figures below.

**The design.** On 15 February 2024, the Court unanimously struck down [[4]](https://www.scobserver.in/cases/association-for-democratic-reforms-electoral-bonds-case-background/) the scheme. According to ADR's report of the judgment, the lead opinion said the absolute non-disclosure of the source of political funding through electoral bonds promoted corruption, and a culture of quid pro quo with the ruling party [[5]](https://adrindia.org/content/supreme-court-declares-electoral-bonds-scheme-unconstitutional). There was also a built-in imbalance:
- Donors had to complete identity checks (KYC) with SBI. KYC documents, PAN, details of identity, and address in full of the donor are recorded by the SBI [[6]](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-indian-express/20240308/281706914644849), which the government itself admitted.
- SBI is government-owned. Voters and opposition parties could not see the donors, but the state-owned bank held the records.

Picture a sealed-bid auction where only one bidder's friend holds the key to the box. You don't need proof that he opened it to see that the game was unfair. I couldn't confirm that the government actually looked at donor data.

**Bribery or extortion: alleged, not proven.** Activists claimed that forty-one companies facing probe by the CBI, ED and the I-T Department gave ~2,471 crore to the BJP through electoral bonds, and ~1,698 crore of it was donated after raids [[7]](https://www.pressreader.com/india/business-standard/20240323/281895893236692). But the Supreme Court refused to order a special investigation (SIT), saying there were only assumptions at this stage on the alleged quid pro quo instances [[8]](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/electoral-bonds-supreme-court-rejects-plea-for-sit-investigation-into-quid-pro-quo-use-of-bonds-by-political-parties-439891-2024-08-02). The Karnataka High Court also quashed the FIR registered against Union Minister Nirmala Sitharaman and former state BJP president Nalin Kumar Kateel [[9]](https://english.publictv.in/karnataka-hc-quashes-fir-against-nirmala-sitharaman-others-in-electoral-bonds-case/) in a complaint alleging extortion through bonds.

## The other side

**The BJP's own defence.** Amit Shah argued that "electoral bonds were introduced to end the dominance of black money in politics" [[10]](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/electoral-bonds-not-black-money-whats-the-ruckus-about-asks-amit-shah-600883/), and that the BJP got Rs 6,000 crore despite having 303 MPs and the rest of the parties have got Rs 14,000 crore against 242 MPs [[10]](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/electoral-bonds-not-black-money-whats-the-ruckus-about-asks-amit-shah-600883/).

Testing that defence:
- **The headline numbers are wrong.** Fact-checkers found the total was Rs. 16,492 crores and not Rs. 20,000 crores [[11]](https://factly.in/the-bjp-accumulated-half-of-the-total-donations-of-rs-16488-2-crore-received-via-electoral-bonds/). Shah set a 2019–24 BJP figure against an inflated total, which makes the BJP's share look like 30% when it was about 47–50%.
- **The per-MP argument partly holds with correct figures.** With 2019–24 data, the BJP got ₹6,060.5 cr ÷ 303 ≈ ₹20 cr per MP. Everyone else got ₹6,708.9 cr ÷ 242 ≈ ₹27.7 cr per MP. That suggests the money follows whoever holds power, at any level.
- **The BJP leads every funding channel, not just bonds.** One study found that between 2016–17 and 2021–22 the BJP took about 66% of non-bond donations, and of corporate donations, the BJP cornered 71% [[12]](https://www.theindiaforum.in/politics/electoral-bonds-incumbency-advantage-and-concentration-political-power). The same study found non-BJP parties have comparative advantage in bond funding [[12]](https://www.theindiaforum.in/politics/electoral-bonds-incumbency-advantage-and-concentration-political-power). So bonds didn't create the BJP's money lead.

**Applying the same test to other parties.** The suspicious-timing argument applies to them too. Future Gaming was the biggest donor for TMC, donating Rs 542 crore [[13]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/elections/lok-sabha-election/electoral-bonds-data-here-s-how-much-the-bjp-congress-and-tmc-received-124032200760_1.html), and the company faced ED raids. That's about a third, so most of that money went to other parties. If timing alone proves extortion by the BJP, it would equally "prove" wrongdoing by the parties that took the rest. It proves neither. It is a pattern that deserves a proper investigation.

## Verdict

**Partly supported.**
- **Unfair design benefiting the party in power: Supported (High confidence).** The BJP was the largest recipient by far. The Supreme Court unanimously struck the scheme down, citing quid-pro-quo risk with the ruling party. And only the state-owned bank held the donor records.
- **The BJP extorted or bribed companies through bonds: Not proven (Low confidence either way).** No court has found this. The Supreme Court called the claims assumptions at this stage, and a criminal case was quashed.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

Probably not. A salary that doubled means **twice as many [dollars/rupees/whatever]**, not twice as much you can buy. To know how much better off you are, you have to take out the rise in prices first.

*I don't know your country, so I've worked through two examples (US and India). The detail that changes the answer most is your country's inflation from 2014 to 2024.*

## The simple formula

**Real gain = (how much your salary multiplied) ÷ (how much prices multiplied)**

Think of your salary as a bag of groceries, not a pile of notes. If you get twice the notes but each grocery item costs 1.5 times as much, your bag is only about a third bigger.

## Example 1: United States

Based on the official Consumer Price Index (CPI-U, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics), the yearly average index was 236.7 in 2014 and 313.7 in 2024 [[1]](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/about-us/monetary-policy/inflation-calculator/consumer-price-index-1913-).

- Prices multiplied by: 313.7 ÷ 236.7 = **1.325** (up about 32.5%)
- Your real gain: 2 ÷ 1.325 = **1.51**

So in the US, a doubled salary buys about **51% more**, not 100% more.

## Example 2: India

One calculator using the national CPI says ₹100 in 2014 equals ₹165.15 in 2024 [[2]](https://www.inflationtool.com/indian-rupee/2014-to-present-value) money.

- Prices multiplied by: **1.65**
- Your real gain: 2 ÷ 1.65 = **1.21**

So in India, a doubled salary buys about **21% more**. India's statistics office has since moved its index to a new base year (2024=100), and published linking factors between the CPI 2012 and CPI 2024 series [[3]](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2227012&reg=3&lang=1). Depending on which series you use, the answer shifts by a percentage point or two. Either way it's roughly a fifth, not double. These India figures come from calculator websites, not straight from the statistics office, so treat them as close estimates.

**In a country with high inflation**, where prices more than doubled over those ten years, a doubled salary would leave you **poorer** than in 2014.

## Three more things the formula misses

1. **Your own costs may differ from the average.** The CPI tracks a typical household's spending. If your rent, school fees or medical bills rose faster than the average, you gained less than the formula says. If they rose more slowly, you gained more.
2. **Tax.** A higher salary can push you into a higher tax bracket, so your take-home pay may not have doubled even if your salary did. Check payslips from both years, not just the headline salary.
3. **Income is not wealth.** "Rich" means what you *own* (savings, property, investments) minus what you *owe*. A bigger salary only makes you richer if some of it turns into savings or assets. Also, if everyone around you got similar raises, your standard of living compared with others may not have changed much.

## Verdict

The idea that a doubled salary means you're twice as rich: **not supported** (high confidence). In both examples above, your real buying power went up, but by far less than double: about +51% in the US and about +21% in India.

- Compare your **take-home pay**, not your gross salary.
- Write down what your five biggest costs were in 2014 and what they are now. That's your personal inflation rate.

But it was not the main reason inflation rose afterwards, and the 2025 GST cuts actually pulled inflation down.

## What is claimed
The claim is that bringing in GST pushed up India's consumer inflation, the CPI (Consumer Price Index), which measures how fast shop prices rise.

## What the evidence shows

**1. What the RBI expected beforehand.** The RBI (India's central bank) said almost 50 per cent of the CPI is expected to be exempt [[1]](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=16505), mostly unprocessed food. It warned GST could also produce a short-lived pass-through to the Inflation trajectory [[1]](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=16505), meaning prices could rise briefly as businesses passed the tax on.

**2. Inflation did rise after July 2017, but from a record low.** The previous low inflation was 1.46 per cent in June 2017. [[2]](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/retail-inflation-slows-to-18-month-low-of-2-19-pc-in-dec-119011400702_1.html) By December, inflation hit a 17-month high at 5.21 per cent [[3]](https://www.india.com/business/retail-inflation-jumps-to-5-21-per-cent-in-december-factory-output-shoots-to-8-4-per-cent-in-november-2840718/).

**3. Other things drove most of that rise:**
- **Vegetables:** Vegetables prices grew 29.13 percent in December [[4]](https://www.oneindia.com/india/india-s-retail-inflation-increases-5-21-december-2619007.html).
- **A government pay decision:** core inflation rose on the back of stronger housing inflation caused by the impact of the central government's house rent allowance (HRA) hike [[5]](https://www.focus-economics.com/countries/india/news/inflation/consumer-inflation-continues-to-accelerate-in-december-wholesale/).
- **Timing:** much of the jump came in October–December, months after GST began. That fits the food and rent shocks better than GST.

**4. Studies disagree on how much GST itself added:**
- One NIPFP (National Institute of Public Finance and Policy) working paper reports earlier research that found no significant increase in inflation after the introduction of GST [[6]](https://nipfp.org.in/media/medialibrary/2022/12/SJ.pdf.pdf) over one year.
- Another NIPFP paper found that with the implementation of GST, WPI inflation increased by 4.16 PPS [[7]](https://nipfp.org.in/media/medialibrary/2022/12/SD.pdf.pdf). WPI tracks wholesale prices, not shop prices. The authors add this perhaps indicates that firms have increased prices post GST implementation [[7]](https://nipfp.org.in/media/medialibrary/2022/12/SD.pdf.pdf).
- Summaries of a study, which I could not trace to the original tables, say with the implementation of GST, CPI increased by 1.37 percentage points [[8]](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/impact-of-gst-on-inflation/). They also say the effect was mixed: higher for paan, tobacco and intoxicants, clothing and footwear, housing, and miscellaneous sectors (mainly consisting of services) [[9]](https://www.adda247.com/upsc-exam/5-years-of-gst/), but lower for non-exempt food, by 4.42% on price levels [[9]](https://www.adda247.com/upsc-exam/5-years-of-gst/).

**5. The 2025 GST cuts lowered inflation.** From September 22, most essential FMCG items will have a GST rate of 5 per cent from the earlier rate of 12-18 per cent. ## The other side

## Verdict
**Not supported as a broad claim. Partly supported as a small, one-time, disputed effect on some goods and services in 2017. Confidence: Medium.**

Think of it as a one-time step up the stairs, not an escalator. A tax change can lift prices once, which raises the yearly inflation figure for about 12 months, and then the effect fades. Most of the 2017 rise came from vegetables and the rent allowance hike.

**What would change this verdict:** an official breakdown by the RBI or MoSPI (the statistics ministry) showing how much of the 2017–18 rise came from GST. I did not find one.

**Counter-Tweet:** Mostly false. India's inflation rose from 1.46% (Jun 2017) to 5.21% (Dec 2017) after GST, but vegetables and a govt rent-allowance hike drove most of it. Studies differ on GST's share. The 2025 GST cuts helped push CPI down to 0.25%.

## What is claimed

## What the evidence shows

**The loss figure is too high for UPI alone.** The Finance Ministry told the Lok Sabha that UPI-related frauds stood at Rs 981 crore in FY 2024–25, involving 12.64 lakh incidents. - Cases: 12.64 lakh ÷ 18,586.6 crore transactions ≈ **1 fraud for every 1.47 lakh transactions**.

Scammers trick individuals into revealing their UPI PIN, OTP, or bank details [[4]](https://razorpay.com/blog/upi-frauds-types-tactics/), or trick them into approving a payment. The key fact: receiving money doesn't require a PIN. [[4]](https://razorpay.com/blog/upi-frauds-types-tactics/) (This comes from industry guides, not an RBI document. ## Verdict
**The premise is partly wrong. The conclusion is not supported.** Confidence: **Medium-High.**

3. **Report fast.** RBI rules give you zero liability when the fraud is a third party breach where the deficiency lies neither with the bank nor with the customer [[8]](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=11040), provided you tell your bank within three working days of receiving the communication from the bank [[8]](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=11040). I couldn't confirm exactly how banks treat those cases.
4. **Call 1930** (the national cyber-fraud helpline) straight away. The government says the system behind it has saved more than ₹11,158 crore across more than 32.80 lakh complaints through June 30, 2026 [[9]](https://www.scanmyshadow.com/blog/2026/10/india-1930-helpline-money-saved-and-upi-fraud-data.html). ## What is claimed
Your bank charged ₹23 for a withdrawal after your free ones ran out, and you want to know if that breaks the rules.

## What the evidence shows

## When the charge *would* be wrong

## The other side
The RBI didn't give a formal reason. Analysts point to rising operating costs, ATM maintenance, and inflationary pressures [[5]](https://www.jurishour.in/notification/rbi-allows-banks-to-hike-atm-fees/). The increase follows requests from white-label ATM operators, who sought higher withdrawal charges due to increasing operational costs [[6]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/topic/atm-cash-withdrawal). You can think the fee is too high, but it's a policy question, not cheating.

## Verdict
**The claim that the charge is illegal is not supported. Confidence: High**, assuming it was a successful withdrawal after your free ones ran out. It would change if the charge came from a failed withdrawal, if you hadn't actually used up your free quota, or if the fee before tax was above ₹23.

## What you can do
- **Count your ATM uses for that month**, including balance checks at other banks' ATMs, and note whether each ATM was in a metro city.
- **If you think the charge is wrong, complain to your bank in writing** with the date, time and ATM location.
- **If the bank says no, or doesn't reply within 30 days,** you can file a free complaint on the RBI's portal, https://cms.rbi.org.in [[7]](https://tgcab.bank.in/public/img/rbios/RBI_CMS_Complaint_Lodging_Process.pdf). The RBI Ombudsman helpline is 14448 [[7]](https://tgcab.bank.in/public/img/rbios/RBI_CMS_Complaint_Lodging_Process.pdf). Some websites say the deadline for filing changed in 2026, but I couldn't confirm that from an RBI document, so file quickly.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

**Short answer: no. I found no evidence that the US government is faking its CPI inflation numbers. I did find real evidence that the data has become less precise since 2025.** Those are two different problems, and it helps to keep them apart.

## What is claimed

The claim is that the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) deliberately reports lower inflation than people actually face. It has been made for years against governments of both parties. The best-known version comes from the website ShadowStats. Since 2025, a newer worry is political pressure on the BLS.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The latest official number is high and rising.** Prices rose 3.7% in the 12 months to September 2026, up from 3.4% in the 12 months to August. [[1]](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm) Gasoline rose 9.1% in September alone, more than half of that month's increase. [[1]](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm) If someone were rigging the index to make the economy look good, a rise like this would be a strange result.

**2. Private trackers don't show hidden higher inflation. Mostly they show the opposite.** Truflation, a private index, builds its numbers from market prices from 30+ independent sources, not government surveys. [[2]](https://truflation.com/marketplace/us-inflation-rate) In 2026 it has often been lower than the official figure. For example, it read about 2.26% to 2.33% for September 2026 [[3]](https://cryptobriefing.com/truflation-us-cpi-2-percent-bls-3-percent/), while the official CPI for August 2026 was 3.4%. [[3]](https://cryptobriefing.com/truflation-us-cpi-2-percent-bls-3-percent/) The main reason for the gap is how each index handles housing costs. [[4]](https://inflationdata.com/articles/2026/04/13/truflation-compared-to-cpi/) The CPI gives housing about one-third of its weight; Truflation gives it closer to one-quarter. [[5]](https://www.heritage.org/markets-and-finance/commentary/less-1-percent-inflation-yes)

**3. The famous "real inflation" figures don't hold up.** Economist James Hamilton reported that the ShadowStats founder told him privately that the site does not actually recalculate BLS data, rather, the Shadowstats CPI merely adds a constant to the officially reported numbers [[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadowstats.com) (via Wikipedia's summary). One analysis found that using ShadowStats figures to adjust GDP would mean the US economy produced no more than it did in 1990 [[7]](https://tsi-blog.com/2015/04/the-official-cpi-versus-the-shadowstats-com-cpi/), which is plainly false.

**4. The real problem: the data has become noisier.**
- In 2025 the BLS stopped collecting CPI data in three metro areas and cut the sample by 15% in the other 72 areas because it lacked resources. [[8]](https://casten.house.gov/media/press-releases/casten-demands-bls-ensure-high-quality-non-partisan-economic-data) According to Reuters, economists blamed years of underfunding under both Republican and Democratic administrations [[9]](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2025/08/12/breaking-news/cheaper-gas-holds-down-u-s-consumer-prices-core-inflation-heats-up/), made worse by the current administration's cuts.
- So more prices are now estimated instead of collected. When a local price is missing, the BLS fills the gap using prices from other areas or related items (called "different-cell" imputation). BLS's own table shows this was 37–40% of estimated prices each month from January to June 2026. [[10]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/imputation-cs-data.htm) In July 2024 it was only 10%. [[9]](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2025/08/12/breaking-news/cheaper-gas-holds-down-u-s-consumer-prices-core-inflation-heats-up/)
- Economists said at the time that these methods would not bias the CPI, but the extra volatility was a concern. [[11]](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2025/0812/1528105-us-inflation-figures/)

**5. The shutdown did make one part of the index look too low for a while, and then it reversed.** The BLS could not collect October 2025 data. [[12]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi.htm) So October rents were carried forward from April 2025, meaning the rent indexes showed no change that month. [[12]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi.htm) BLS research later suggested housing inflation may have been understated by roughly 0.3% to 0.6% year-over-year during that stretch. [[13]](https://www.mortgagenewsdaily.com/news/05152026-cpi-shelter-shutdown) The effect reversed in April 2026 when the same homes were surveyed again. [[14]](https://perc.tamu.edu/blog/2026/06/shelter-cpi.html) In that April report, the yearly rate jumped from 3.3% to 3.8%. [[15]](https://www.economy.com/united-states/cpi-urban-consumer-owners-equivalent-rent-of-residences/seasonally-adjusted) Think of a bathroom scale that skipped a weigh-in: the missing weight shows up all at once later.

**6. Political pressure is real.** On August 1, 2025, President Trump fired the BLS head [[16]](https://www.csmonitor.com/Editorials/the-monitors-view/2026/0715/Shoring-up-US-data-integrity-and-credibility) and claimed the numbers were "rigged," which experts on Wall Street and in academia widely disputed. [[16]](https://www.csmonitor.com/Editorials/the-monitors-view/2026/0715/Shoring-up-US-data-integrity-and-credibility) His first replacement pick, Heritage Foundation economist and BLS critic E.J. Antoni, worried some economists [[9]](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2025/08/12/breaking-news/cheaper-gas-holds-down-u-s-consumer-prices-core-inflation-heats-up/). In the end the Senate confirmed longtime BLS economist Brett Matsumoto on August 7, 2026. [[17]](https://www.c2er.org/2026/08/senate-confirms-brett-matsumoto-to-lead-bls/) The commissioner is the only political appointee in an agency of about 2,000 people. [[18]](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/brett-matsumoto-confirmed-as-bls-commissioner-after-predecessor-fired-93CH-4847326)

## The other side

**The BLS's own defence:** for October 2025, it used its existing, documented imputation method and did not depart from the methods in its handbook. [[12]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi.htm) It says writing and testing a new method would have delayed the November 2025 CPI and later releases considerably. [[12]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi.htm) At his confirmation hearing, Matsumoto said methodology changes would be approved by career staff and explained publicly with supporting research. [[17]](https://www.c2er.org/2026/08/senate-confirms-brett-matsumoto-to-lead-bls/)

**Independent watchdogs agree there was no manipulation.** Friends of BLS, a group focused on the accuracy of government data, said a disputed housing figure in the November 2025 report was a "wording error" rather than political manipulation. [[19]](https://www.scotsmanguide.com/news/friends-of-bls-dismisses-political-interference-claims-in-cpi-controversy/)

**Where the skeptics have a point:** a CPI built on fewer real price checks is less reliable. And the CPI measures an *average* household. Your costs can be very different. In September 2026, prices in the New York area were up 4.5% [[20]](https://www.bls.gov/regions/northeast/news-release/consumerpriceindex_newyork.htm), compared with 3.4% in the South. [[21]](https://www.bls.gov/regions/southeast/news-release/consumerpriceindex_south.htm) If you drive a lot, energy prices up 20.4% in a year [[1]](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm) hit you much harder than the average suggests.

**Same test for both sides:** I would give the same verdict whichever party was in power. ShadowStats-style claims were aimed at earlier administrations. Worries about the current one come mostly from its critics. In both cases there is no evidence of fabricated numbers, and the funding cuts that weakened the data happened under both parties.

## Verdict

- **"The CPI is faked": not supported. Confidence: medium-high.** The official numbers are rising, private trackers don't point to hidden higher inflation, and the best-known "real inflation" alternative falls apart when checked.
- **"CPI data quality has gotten worse": supported. Confidence: high.** Smaller samples, far more estimated prices, and a temporary shutdown distortion are all documented.
- **What would change my view:** an inspector-general finding or whistleblower evidence of tampering, method changes made without career staff and published research, or a lasting, unexplained gap between the CPI and both private indexes and other official price measures. The Labor Department's inspector general announced a review of the challenges the BLS faces with CPI, producer price and jobs data [[22]](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/1180473408). I could not find its results.

## What you can do

- **Check your own area.** BLS publishes CPI for regions and big metro areas, which may be closer to what you actually pay.
- **Compare sources.** Look at private trackers alongside the official CPI. If they suddenly started running well above it for months, that would be a real warning sign.
- **Watch the quality signals.** BLS publishes how many prices it estimates each month. If that keeps rising, the monthly figure is less trustworthy.
- **If your income is tied to CPI** (pensions, benefits, rent clauses), a noisier index can move your payments by a few tenths of a percent in a given year. That's worth knowing, but it's not fraud.

**Counter-Tweet:** Think CPI is faked? Check for yourself: BLS publishes regional CPI and how many prices it estimates each month. Private trackers like Truflation have mostly run BELOW official CPI in 2026, not above. The real issue is less precise data, not made-up numbers.

### 13. Global consumer
**Q:** Are Nigerian banks allowed to charge me for every transfer I make?
**Strength:** It separates the CBN-capped bank fee from the ₦50 stamp duty. It openly flags that the 2026 Guide may still be an unfinalised exposure draft and shows what each version means in practice. Its worked examples are arithmetically correct, and the complaint route (bank in writing, then CBN Consumer Protection with contact details) is concrete and sourced.

**Weakness:** The verdict label 'Partly supported, with high confidence' fits a yes/no legal question poorly and sits awkwardly beside the admitted uncertainty over whether the 2026 caps are in force. The answer leans on secondary news reports rather than the CBN Guide itself. The 'other side' gives only official justifications and omits banks' cost-recovery case and criticisms of the caps. Intrabank transfers and the ₦5,000 boundary gap in the 2020 bands are not addressed.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

Assuming this is a personal account and you mean electronic transfers (app, internet banking or USSD) from a Nigerian bank to another account:

**Short answer: Yes, banks are allowed to charge for most transfers, but only up to limits set by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN).** A transfer of ₦10,000 or more can also carry a separate ₦50 government stamp duty. If you're paying more than these limits, that's an overcharge and you can ask for a refund.

## What is claimed
The question is whether charging you on every transfer is lawful. It comes down to two things: the bank's own fee, and a government tax that the bank collects.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The bank's transfer fee (capped by the CBN)**

- **2020 Guide (the last rules I could confirm as final):** From January 1, 2020, electronic funds transfers cost N10 below N5,000, N25 from N5,001 to N50,000, and N50 above N50,000. [[1]](https://businessday.ng/banking-finance/article/cbns-guide-to-bank-charges-stipulates-n2m-penalty-per-infraction/)
- **2026 Guide (lower fees):** Interbank transfers are capped at N10 from N5,000 to N50,000 and N50 above N50,000. Transfers below N5,000 are free. [[2]](https://nairametrics.com/2026/04/23/cbn-caps-bank-fees-mandates-transparency-in-proposed-guidelines/) Proshare reported May 1, 2026 as the date for full industry compliance. [[3]](https://proshare.co/articles/cbn-releases-updated-guide-to-bank-charges-to-enhance-transparency-and-consumer-protection)
- **Unclear: is the 2026 Guide final?** CBN issued it as an "Exposure Draft" [[4]](https://regalert.substack.com/p/alert-central-bank-of-nigeria-published-86f), and comments were due by May 08, 2026, after the planned start date of May 01, 2026. [[4]](https://regalert.substack.com/p/alert-central-bank-of-nigeria-published-86f) Some outlets report it as in force. One October article says "we did not find a final 2026 guide published by the CBN." [[5]](https://thegazette.ng/bank-charges-nigeria-2026-cbn-guide/) I couldn't find a final version on the CBN site either. In practice: a fee above ₦50 on a single transfer breaks both versions. A ₦10 fee on a ₦3,000 transfer is fine under the 2020 rules but not under the 2026 draft.
- **The CBN's 2020 rules come with penalties.** Under the 2020 guide, a bank that breaks it can be fined N2,000,000 per breach. [[6]](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2020/02/bank-comply-with-reduction-in-charges/amp/)

**2. The ₦50 stamp duty (a government tax, not a bank fee)**

- From January 1, 2026, banks charge senders ₦50 stamp duty on electronic transfers of ₦10,000 or more, under the new Tax Act. [[7]](https://www.thecable.ng/banks-to-start-charging-senders-n50-stamp-duty-on-transfers-above-n10k-from-january/)
- Salary payments and transfers between your own accounts at the same bank are exempt. [[7]](https://www.thecable.ng/banks-to-start-charging-senders-n50-stamp-duty-on-transfers-above-n10k-from-january/) That's according to a UBA customer email reported by TheCable.
- Before this, the ₦50 levy was usually taken from the person receiving the money. [[8]](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/12/31/banks-to-effect-%E2%82%A650-stamp-duty-on-transfers-above-%E2%82%A610k-from-january-1/) So the tax isn't new. What changed is that the sender now pays it.

**Worked examples (my own calculations):**
- Send ₦3,000: no stamp duty. Bank fee is ₦10 under the 2020 rules, ₦0 under the 2026 draft.
- Send ₦20,000 to another bank: ₦50 stamp duty plus a bank fee of ₦25 (2020) or ₦10 (2026 draft). Total ₦75 or ₦60.
- Send ₦200,000: ₦50 + ₦50 = ₦100 under either version.

## The other side
- **The CBN says** its fee limits are meant "to encourage financial inclusion and to reduce the burden of bank charges on consumers." [[1]](https://businessday.ng/banking-finance/article/cbns-guide-to-bank-charges-stipulates-n2m-penalty-per-infraction/)
- **The government's position:** President Tinubu said the tax reforms are meant to overhaul the system, not increase the burden on citizens. [[9]](https://www.newsghana.com.gh/nigerian-banks-to-charge-n50-stamp-duty-on-transfers-from-january-2026/)
- **My view:** a flat ₦50 tax hits small senders hardest in proportion. It's 0.5% of a ₦10,000 transfer but only 0.005% of a ₦1 million one. That's legal, but it's a policy choice you can criticise.

## Verdict
**Partly supported, with high confidence.** Banks may charge for most transfers, but only within CBN limits. Small transfers (under ₦5,000) may now be free if the 2026 Guide is in force. The ₦50 stamp duty is a lawful tax, not bank profit. A final CBN-published 2026 Guide, or one from your own bank, would settle the remaining question about small transfers.

## What you can do
1. **Check your debit alerts.** The CBN requires every alert for a charge to describe what the charge is. [[10]](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/money/1651095-all-cbn-approved-bank-charges-savings-current-account/) Unexplained debits are a red flag.
2. **Complain to your bank first, in writing,** and keep the reference number. If the bank hasn't dealt with it within two weeks, you can escalate to the CBN's Consumer Protection Department. [[11]](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/12/19/cbn-moves-to-protect-bank-customers-against-excess-charges-illegal-debits/)
3. **Escalate to the CBN:** email cpd@cbn.gov.ng or contactcbn@cbn.gov.ng, or call +234 7002255226. [[11]](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2021/12/19/cbn-moves-to-protect-bank-customers-against-excess-charges-illegal-debits/) Attach your statement and proof that you complained to the bank. The CBN does not charge any fee for this. [[12]](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/FinInc/FinLit/CPD_FAQs.pdf)
4. **Watch out for scams.** Be wary of anyone on social media offering to "fast-track" a CBN complaint for a fee. [[13]](https://www.mypryme.com/blog/how-to-complain-to-cbn-consumer-protection)

**Counter-Tweet:** Nigerian bank transfer fees are capped at ₦50, and the CBN's 2026 draft makes transfers under ₦5,000 free. A separate ₦50 stamp duty applies from ₦10,000. Overcharged? Complain to your bank, then cpd@cbn.gov.ng. It's free.

### 14. Market claim
**Q:** A WhatsApp group promises 5% guaranteed monthly returns from crypto trading. Should I invest?
**Strength:** The compounding maths is correct and checkable: 1.05^12 ≈ 1.80, 1,000 grows to about 18,700 over 5 years and about 349,000 over 10. The answer pairs this with regulator red-flag language on 'guaranteed' and 'overly consistent' returns, a Ponzi explanation of why early payouts prove nothing, a clear refusal to give personal advice, and practical reporting and recovery-scam warnings.

**Weakness:** Some source attributions are loose. 'In the US, the SEC says to check... Investor.gov' is cited to a CFTC page. 'Regulators flag wiring money...' is cited to a media outlet (cryptoslate) rather than a regulator. The $15.3M figure sits alongside a source titled '$14 million trap' without explaining the gap. The specific case details come from secondary reporting, not the SEC complaint itself. Reporting steps are generic and do not name non-US regulators.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

I can't give personal investment advice, but I can show you what the evidence says about this kind of offer. In short, a "guaranteed 5% a month from crypto trading" pitch on WhatsApp matches the exact pattern US regulators warn about again and again. I'm assuming you were added to the group or contacted without asking. If you tell me your country, I can point you to the right place to report it.

## What is claimed
A WhatsApp group says it can earn you a **guaranteed** 5% a month by trading crypto.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The maths doesn't work.** 5% a month, compounded, is about **80% a year** (1.05¹² ≈ 1.80). Over 5 years, 1,000 would become about 18,700. Over 10 years, about 349,000. Anyone who could really do that would quickly become one of the richest people alive. They wouldn't need strangers' money on WhatsApp.

**2. "Guaranteed" is the warning sign.** The US securities and commodities regulators (the SEC and CFTC) say all investments have risk, and investors should question any so-called "guaranteed" return. [[1]](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/watch_out_for_digital_fraud.html) They also say claims such as "risk-free", "zero risk", "absolutely safe", and "guaranteed profit" are hallmarks of a fraud. [[1]](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/watch_out_for_digital_fraud.html) Crypto prices rise and fall sharply, so a fixed monthly payout makes no sense for real trading.

**3. Steady returns are another warning sign.** The SEC lists overly consistent returns [[2]](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/investor-7) as a red flag, because investments tend to go up and down over time, especially those seeking high returns. [[2]](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/investor-7)

**4. This exact setup has been charged recently.** In September 2026, the SEC charged two offshore entities with defrauding over 2,000 retail investors of at least $15.3 million through fake AI-powered crypto trading platforms. [[3]](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/criminal-law/sec-charges-whatsapp-driven-crypto-fraud-schemes-totaling-over-15-million) These are allegations, not court rulings. According to the SEC's complaint, the schemes ran through WhatsApp group chats, impersonated investment professionals, and fabricated regulatory filings [[3]](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/criminal-law/sec-charges-whatsapp-driven-crypto-fraud-schemes-totaling-over-15-million). Withdrawals were blocked with demands for more money. The SEC alleges there were no bots and that deposited funds were never used to generate returns. [[4]](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/09/314375-sec-alleges-that-whatsapp-group-chats-and-fake-filings-were-used-in-15m-crypto-ai-scams/)

**5. How people end up in these groups.** The CFTC explains that by default, messaging apps allow anyone with your phone number to call or add you to a discussion group. [[5]](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9005-24) Scammers use this to add random numbers.

## The other side
Groups like this usually say they have expert traders or an AI bot. They post screenshots of profits, and early members may really get paid. That's not proof the trading is real. In a Ponzi scheme, purported returns to existing investors [[2]](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/investor-7) are paid from funds contributed by new investors. [[2]](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/investor-7) Think of it as a bucket filled from the top and leaking from the bottom: it looks full until new money stops coming in. Happy members can also be fake. Reporting on the SEC case describes how fake fellow "investors" celebrate paper gains [[4]](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/09/314375-sec-alleges-that-whatsapp-group-chats-and-fake-filings-were-used-in-15m-crypto-ai-scams/).

## Verdict
**The claim that a 5% monthly return can be guaranteed is not supported. Confidence: High.** I can't prove that this particular group is a scam without its name and details. But the offer shows the main warning signs regulators list.

What would change my view: proof that the operator is registered with your national financial regulator (checked on the regulator's own website, not a screenshot), independently audited accounts, and dropping the word "guaranteed."

## What you can do
- **Don't send money**, and especially not crypto to a wallet address they give you. Regulators flag wiring money to individuals or sending crypto to unknown wallets as a key sign of fraud. - **If you've already paid:** contact your bank or exchange right away. Ignore anyone who asks for a "fee" or "tax" to release your money, or who offers to recover your losses for a fee. Those are usually second scams.
