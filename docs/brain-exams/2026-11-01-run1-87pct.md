<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

The question rests on a false premise. The RBI did not ban UPI merchant fees in 2023. The fee ban came from the government in 2020, and in 2023 a new fee was actually *added* for one type of UPI payment.

## What is claimed
That the Reserve Bank of India stopped all UPI transaction fees for merchants in 2023.

## What the evidence shows
**1. It was the government, not the RBI, and it was 2020.** The zero merchant discount rate regime was rolled out in January 2020 for RuPay Debit Card and BHIM-UPI transactions through amendments in Section 10A of the Payments and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 and Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act, 1961. [[1]](https://inc42.com/features/end-of-free-upi-why-fintech-startups-want-mdr/) The stated reason, according to Forbes India, was that the government removed MDR on UPI and RuPay debit cards to push mass adoption of digital payments. [[2]](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/what-is-merchant-discount-rate-on-upi-and-why-does-india-want-to-bring-it-back/2996894/1) Before that, a merchant discount rate (MDR) of up to 0.30 percent was applicable for UPI person-to-merchant transactions. [[2]](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/what-is-merchant-discount-rate-on-upi-and-why-does-india-want-to-bring-it-back/2996894/1)

**2. The RBI actually raised the question of charging.** In August 2022 the RBI published a discussion paper, which included this line: "In any economic activity, including payment systems, there does not seem to be any justification for a free service, unless there is an element of public good" [[3]](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/govt-not-considering-levying-charges-for-upi-services-clarifies-finance-ministry-345176-2022-08-22). The RBI also reiterated it has neither taken any view nor has any specific opinion on the issues raised in the discussion paper. [[4]](https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/no-plans-to-levy-any-charges-for-upi-payments-says-finmin-122082100811_1.html) The Finance Ministry then said there is no consideration in Govt to levy any charges for UPI services. [[4]](https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/no-plans-to-levy-any-charges-for-upi-payments-says-finmin-122082100811_1.html)

**3. In 2023 a fee was introduced.** On March 24, the National Payments Corporation of India (NCPI) issued a circular saying that Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions of above INR 2,000 made through prepaid payment instruments (PPI) to merchants will attract an interchange fee of 1.1%. [[5]](https://inc42.com/buzz/explained-npcis-interchange-fee-for-ppi-based-upi-transactions/) That applies to wallet payments only. NPCI clarified that linking the bank account in any UPI-enabled app [[6]](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/no-charge-on-normal-upi-payment-interchange-fee-for-ppi-transactions-npci-123032900477_1.html) was the method behind over 99.9% of UPI transactions, and that these bank account-to-account transactions continue to remain free for customers and merchants. [[6]](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/no-charge-on-normal-upi-payment-interchange-fee-for-ppi-transactions-npci-123032900477_1.html) So "free UPI" carried on in 2023, but it wasn't a new RBI ban.

**4. Where things stand now (October 2026).** The free regime is being partly undone. The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026 changed this mechanism [[7]](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-upi-mdr-rs-2000-threshold/), which lets the government choose which payment modes stay protected. In September, the NPCI announced that a 0.4 per cent Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) will be levied on person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions above Rs 2,000 [[8]](https://www.manoramayearbook.in/current-affairs/india/2026/10/14/mdr-on-upi-explained.html). On October 13, NPCI said a new date for the implementation will be decided after Diwali. [[9]](https://www.dtnext.in/news/national/npci-defers-levy-of-mdr-on-upi-transactions-no-new-date-announced-for-implementation) NPCI has not scrapped the proposed MDR. [[10]](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/upi-mdr-rollout-deferred-npci-pushes-04-charge-beyond-diwali-id022-59996)

## The other side
The best case for keeping UPI free is the government's own: in its words, UPI is a digital public good with immense convenience for the public & productivity gains for the economy. [[11]](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/no-consideration-to-levy-any-charges-for-upi-services-finance-ministry-1138106.html) Instead of fees, it paid subsidies. The industry's counter-argument is that zero MDR also meant banks, National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) and fintech startups got nothing from the transaction. [[2]](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/what-is-merchant-discount-rate-on-upi-and-why-does-india-want-to-bring-it-back/2996894/1) For the 2026 plan, the Finance Ministry has said that MDR is neither a tax nor a charge collected by the government or NPCI. [[8]](https://www.manoramayearbook.in/current-affairs/india/2026/10/14/mdr-on-upi-explained.html)

## Verdict
**Not supported. High confidence.** All three parts are wrong: who did it (the government and Parliament, not the RBI), when (January 2020, not 2023), and what happened in 2023 (a fee was added for wallet payments, not banned). New evidence would only change this if an RBI circular from 2023 banning merchant fees turned up, and I found none.

## What you can do

Check how your bank's settlement statement shows fees before the new start date is announced.

**Counter-Tweet:** Myth: the RBI banned UPI merchant fees in 2023. Fact: the government made UPI fee-free in Jan 2020. In 2023 NPCI *added* up to 1.1% on wallet payments over ₹2,000. Now a 0.4% merchant fee over ₹2,000 has been announced but delayed until after Diwali.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

Assuming you're in India (the rules are very different in the US, UK and elsewhere, so tell me if you're not):

## What is claimed
The claim is that banks "steal" from customers through minimum balance penalties. Legally, that's mostly wrong: these charges are allowed and written into your account terms. But the claim gets three things right. The fee is badly out of proportion to what you get. It falls hardest on people with the least money. And sometimes banks charge it in ways the rules ban.

## What the evidence shows

**The amount is large.** Banks in India collected ₹7,086.63 crore as penalties for not maintaining the Minimum Average Balance in savings and current accounts during FY 2025–26 [[1]](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/banks-collected-%E2%82%B97086-crore-as-minimum-balance-penalties-in-fy26-government/amp/), according to the Finance Ministry. Over FY23 to FY26 the total was over ₹26,170 crore. [[2]](https://www.ap7am.com/en/132465/banks-collected-rs-26170-crore-in-minimum-balance-charges-over-fy23-fy26-govt) In FY26, HDFC Bank collected the most (₹1,798.14 crore), followed by Axis Bank at ₹1,081.33 crore. [[3]](https://www.thehansindia.com/business/private-banks-collect-rs-4949-crore-in-minimum-balance-penalties-1102537) One caveat: these totals include current (business) accounts. For example, SBI's figure is only for current accounts, because SBI has not charged this penalty on savings accounts since March 2020. [[2]](https://www.ap7am.com/en/132465/banks-collected-rs-26170-crore-in-minimum-balance-charges-over-fy23-fy26-govt) Different reports also give different FY25 figures, probably because they cover different numbers of banks.

**The penalty is far bigger than the reward.** A Parliament committee heard that a customer who keeps the minimum balance earns only the low savings interest rate, while the penalty for missing it is 15 to 20 times that interest [[4]](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/parliament-panel-asks-banks-to-scrap-penalties-on-minimum-balance-rule-3897226). A simple example: say your bank pays 3% a year (check your own rate). Keeping ₹10,000 in the account earns you ₹300 a year, or ₹25 a month. Missing the minimum can cost you many times that. It's like a shop paying you ₹1 to stand in the queue but fining you ₹20 for stepping out of it.

**It hits poorer people hardest.** The committee pointed to hardship for MGNREGA workers and low-income account holders [[4]](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/parliament-panel-asks-banks-to-scrap-penalties-on-minimum-balance-rule-3897226) and asked banks to stop minimum balance penalties [[4]](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/parliament-panel-asks-banks-to-scrap-penalties-on-minimum-balance-rule-3897226).

**Where it becomes against the rules.** Under RBI's 2014 rules:
- The bank must tell you clearly by SMS, email or letter that penalties will apply if the balance isn't restored within a month of the notice [[5]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/English/Scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1500).
- The charge must be a fixed percentage of the gap between your actual balance and the agreed minimum [[5]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/English/Scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1500), not a flat fine.
- The penalty alone must never push your savings balance below zero. [[5]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/English/Scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1500)
- RBI has said banks may not charge this penalty on any inoperative account [[6]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/no-penalty-if-minimum-balance-not-kept-in-dormant-bank-account-114050601209_1.html).

RBI does fine banks that break these rules. For example, it fined a co-operative bank that had charged fixed penalties without one month's notice, charged penalties on inoperative accounts, and charged fees to reactivate inoperative accounts [[7]](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/PressReleases.aspx?Id=3589).

**A common trap:** a salary account usually has no minimum. When you change jobs, the money stops coming in and the bank starts charging normal savings-account penalties [[8]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/your-savings-account-will-no-longer-have-a-negative-balance-report-116051100227_1.html).

## The other side
- **Banks set this themselves, and RBI says that's allowed.** RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said banks decide the minimum balance because it isn't under RBI's regulatory control [[9]](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/icici-bank-faces-backlash-reduces-minimum-average-balance-for-new-savings-accounts-to-rs15000-1.500232421).
- **A free option exists.** About 72 crore basic (BSBD) accounts, including Jan Dhan accounts, never face this penalty. [[10]](https://taxguru.in/rbi/banks-allowed-charge-reasonable-minimum-balance-penalty-government.html)
- **Competition and public pressure do work.** 10 of 12 public sector banks have fully stopped this penalty on savings accounts. [[1]](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/banks-collected-%E2%82%B97086-crore-as-minimum-balance-penalties-in-fy26-government/amp/) When ICICI raised its minimum for new accounts to ₹50,000, it cut it to ₹15,000 after criticism [[11]](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/companies/icici-bank-cuts-minimum-balance-for-new-savings-accounts-to-rs-15000-from-rs-50000-3679495).

These points are fair. But they don't explain why the penalty is so out of proportion. And the free basic account only helps if people know it exists.

## What is claimed
"Unfairly" can mean two things:
1. **The system was rigged.** The BJP got a lopsided benefit from a funding system that was itself unfair.
2. **Crime.** The money was extorted from companies, or paid in return for contracts or relief from raids.

## What the evidence shows

**The scheme was unconstitutional.** On 15 February 2024, ahead of the 2024 Lok Sabha election, the Supreme Court declared unconstitutional the electoral bonds scheme that allowed individuals and companies to make unlimited anonymous donations to political parties. [[1]](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/electoral-bonds-scheme-unconstitutional-should-be-cancelled-supreme-courts-big-verdict-591060) The five-judge bench was unanimous. It said the scheme disproportionately restricts the fundamental right of voters to information about the source of funds accepted by a political party [[2]](https://scroll.in/article/1063790/explained-why-the-supreme-court-struck-down-the-electoral-bonds-scheme).

The court also found the secrecy worked one way only. The bonds gave "selective anonymity" to "economically resourced contributors who already have a seat at the table…vis-à-vis the public and not the political party" [[2]](https://scroll.in/article/1063790/explained-why-the-supreme-court-struck-down-the-electoral-bonds-scheme). In plain words, parties could know who paid them, but voters could not. The court reinstated corporate donation limits, saying that treating companies and individuals alike for this was "manifestly arbitrary" [[3]](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/2/15/indias-supreme-court-scraps-electoral-bonds-calls-it-unconstitutional).

**The BJP got the biggest share.** The party that introduced the scheme received Rs 6,060 crore in electoral bonds, constituting 47% of the total donations, followed by TMC Rs 1,609.50 crore (12.6%) and Congress Rs 1,421.9 crore (11.1%) [[4]](https://www.business-standard.com/elections/lok-sabha-election/electoral-bonds-bjp-received-more-funds-than-the-next-6-parties-combined-124031500434_1.html). Electoral bonds worth over Rs 2,500 crore were sold between March 2018 and March 2019 [[5]](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fbjps-highest-electoral-bond-encashment-recorded-during-2019-lok-sabha-polls-2938955), and that period was not in the release. **The timing of some donations is suspicious.** Journalists found many cases where donations followed raids. **Courts have not confirmed any quid pro quo.** In August 2024, the Supreme Court said it cannot order a roving inquiry into purchase of electoral bonds on the assumption of a quid pro quo for award of contract. I could not settle which is correct.

## The other side
Home Minister Amit Shah argued the scheme was introduced to end the dominance of black money in politics [[13]](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/bonds-not-black-money-whats-the-ruckus-all-about-asks-shah-601010), and that the BJP got Rs 6,000 crore despite having 303 MPs while the other parties got Rs 14,000 crore against 242 MPs. [[14]](https://www.thequint.com/news/webqoof/home-minister-amit-shah-remark-opposition-received-14-thousand-crore-electoral-bonds-fact-check)

Still, part of his argument holds up when tested:

- Using the correct numbers: about ₹6,060 crore ÷ 303 MPs ≈ ₹20 crore per MP. For other parties, about ₹6,086 crore ÷ 242 MPs ≈ ₹25 crore per MP.
- Parties that ruled states also collected large sums. TMC (West Bengal), BRS (Telangana), BJD (Odisha) and DMK (Tamil Nadu) were all among the big receivers.

So the pattern looks like **money flowing to whoever holds power**, at both national and state level. It does not show a uniquely BJP-only machine.

On the raid-then-donate cases, timing alone does not prove cause. Companies being investigated may also donate for other reasons, and some of these firms gave to other parties too. The data shows a pattern worth investigating, not a proven bribe.

**Symmetry test:** if a Congress or TMC government had designed a secret, unlimited donation scheme and taken the largest share, I would reach the same verdict. That is the real problem: whoever holds power gets the advantage.

- **What would change this:** a court-ordered investigation, or documents linking specific donations to specific contracts or dropped cases.

### 7. Nominal vs real
**Q:** My salary doubled from 2014 to 2024, so am I twice as rich now?
**Strength:** It shows the inflation adjustment step by step with correct arithmetic. US CPI-U 236.7 to 313.7 gives 1.325 and a real gain of about 1.51. <details><summary>Full answer</summary>

**Short answer: almost certainly not.** Your salary doubled, but prices went up too. What counts is how much your money can buy, and that depends on your country. You didn't say where you live, so here are two examples, the US and India. Your country's inflation rate is the detail that changes the answer.

## The simple test

**Real gain = (new salary ÷ old salary) ÷ (new prices ÷ old prices)**

Think of your salary as a bucket and prices as a hole in the bottom. Doubling the water you pour in doesn't double what stays in the bucket if the hole got bigger too.

## Worked examples

**United States.** The average US consumer price index (CPI-U) was 236.7 in 2014 and 313.7 in 2024 [[1]](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/about-us/monetary-policy/inflation-calculator/consumer-price-index-1913-).
- Prices: 313.7 ÷ 236.7 = 1.325, so prices rose about 32.5%
- Real gain: 2 ÷ 1.325 = 1.51
- **Your salary buys about 51% more, not 100% more.** That's still a good raise.

**India.** One series built from official statistics office (MoSPI) data shows a CPI average of 61.82 in 2014 and 100.56 in 2024 [[2]](https://www.rateinflation.com/consumer-price-index/india-historical-cpi/).
- Prices: 100.56 ÷ 61.82 = 1.627, so prices rose about 63%
- Real gain: 2 ÷ 1.627 = 1.23
- **Your salary buys only about 23% more.**

A second calculator, using OECD and World Bank figures, says ₹1 in 2014 had the buying power of about ₹1.65 in 2024 [[3]](https://www.officialdata.org/india/inflation/2014?amount=1). That gives a real gain of about 21%, so the two sources roughly agree. Both are secondary sites, not the official tables themselves.

**Countries with high inflation:** if prices more than doubled over the decade, a doubled salary means you can actually buy *less* than in 2014.

## Four more traps

1. **Salary is not wealth.** Salary is money coming in. Wealth is what you own minus what you owe. You can earn twice as much and still have nothing saved.
2. **Your own prices may differ from the average.** The CPI is a national average shopping basket. If your rent, school fees or medical bills rose faster than average, the real gain is smaller for you.
3. **Taxes.** A bigger salary can push you into a higher tax band, so your take-home pay may have grown less than your gross pay. This depends on the country; I didn't check specific tax rules.
4. **Everyone else's pay.** If wages around you also doubled, your position compared with others, and with things like house prices, may not have changed.

## Verdict

"Twice as rich" is **not supported** for any country with normal inflation, which includes almost every country. **Confidence: High.** The only thing that changes is the size of your real gain, and that depends on your country and your own spending.

## What you can do

- Look up your country's CPI for 2014 and 2024 on the national statistics office or central bank website, and use the formula above.
- Use your **take-home** pay, not gross pay, for a truer picture.
- Check your biggest costs (rent, fees, healthcare) against your salary separately. That's where real-life inflation often hides.
- Tell me your country and I'll do the calculation for you.

### 8. Causal claim
**Q:** GST caused inflation in India to go up. True or false?
**Strength:** It lays out the conflicting research clearly: Kumar & Dash find about +1.37 pp on CPI, a 2024 NIPFP paper finds a rise via food, and Jha finds no effect. It cites sources, gives accurate CPI figures (4.5% → 3.6% → 3.4%), and openly notes that low food inflation could mask a GST push.

**Weakness:** The verdict undercuts its own caveat. Two of the three counterfactual studies find a positive effect on headline CPI, and one is called the 'strongest evidence'. Yet the verdict ('mostly false') and the counter-tweet lean on the raw before/after decline in headline inflation. Food is the only other driver named. Fuel and global oil prices, demonetisation's effect on food prices in 2017, and the pandemic are all left out, so the masking argument is never developed. The 2025 rate-cut evidence also does little to settle what the 2017 rollout did.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

**Short answer: mostly false as a general statement, with a small grain of truth.** Overall retail inflation in India went *down* after GST started in July 2017. A few studies find GST pushed up prices of some non-food goods and services, but researchers disagree, and the 2025 GST rate cuts pulled inflation down.

## What is claimed
The claim is that GST (the Goods and Services Tax, launched 1 July 2017) made consumer prices in India rise faster than they otherwise would have.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The headline numbers fell after GST.** According to the government's Economic Survey, headline CPI inflation declined to 3.4 per cent in 2018-19 from 3.6 per cent in 2017-18 [[1]](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2019-20/economicsurvey/doc/vol2chapter/echap04_vol2.pdf), and from 4.5% in 2016-17. The 2018-19 figure was the lowest average since the new series of CPI-C began. [[1]](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2019-20/economicsurvey/doc/vol2chapter/echap04_vol2.pdf) If GST had caused a big price surge, you would expect these numbers to go up, not down.

But there's a catch. The Survey says the fall was mainly due to low food inflation [[1]](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2019-20/economicsurvey/doc/vol2chapter/echap04_vol2.pdf). Cheap food could have hidden a smaller GST push somewhere else. That's why the careful studies matter.

**2. The RBI expected only a small effect.** Before GST started, the RBI said it could produce a short-lived pass-through impact on the inflation trajectory [[2]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/news-ians/gst-rate-will-have-short-lived-impact-on-inflation-rbi-116100400850_1.html). It also said overall price levels may go down due to more efficient allocation of factors of production [[2]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/news-ians/gst-rate-will-have-short-lived-impact-on-inflation-rbi-116100400850_1.html). One reason the effect was expected to be small: according to RBI (2017), 50 per cent of the items under CPI are exempted from GST. [[3]](https://nipfp.org.in/media/medialibrary/2022/12/SJ.pdf.pdf)

**3. The research disagrees:**
- **Found a rise:** A study by Kumar and Dash says actual CPI growth over the period studied was 4.61%. Without the implementation of GST, the CPI growth would have been 3.24%, indicating that, with the implementation of GST, CPI grew by 1.37%. [[4]](https://www.gift.res.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Did_inflation_rise_after_GST_Anoop_S_Kumar__Santosh_Kumar_Dash.pdf) The rise was concentrated in Pan, Tobacco and Intoxicants, Clothing and Footwear, Housing and Miscellaneous sectors. [[4]](https://www.gift.res.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Did_inflation_rise_after_GST_Anoop_S_Kumar__Santosh_Kumar_Dash.pdf) The same study found that in the case of Food, GST reduces the price levels [[4]](https://www.gift.res.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Did_inflation_rise_after_GST_Anoop_S_Kumar__Santosh_Kumar_Dash.pdf).
- **Also found a rise, but through a different channel:** A 2024 NIPFP working paper found a positive effect of GST on headline inflation in India via inflationary impact on retail food prices. [[5]](https://www.nipfp.org.in/publication-index-page/working-paper-index-page/?page=2) So it blames food, where Kumar and Dash found food prices fell.
- **Found nothing:** Another NIPFP-hosted paper concluded there is no impact of GST on Indian inflation at all. [[3]](https://nipfp.org.in/media/medialibrary/2022/12/SJ.pdf.pdf)

**4. The 2025 GST cuts lowered inflation.** SBI Research estimated the drop in CPI inflation from the September 2025 rate cuts at around 25 basis points so far in the Sep-Nov'25 period [[6]](https://www.republicworld.com/business/gst-cuts-trim-cpi-inflation-25-bps-in-sep-nov-sbi-eyes-35-bps-total-fy26-impact) (0.25 percentage points). But a 2026 NIPFP paper found the cuts were not fully passed on for everyday items. The CPI for food, household goods and personal care products has risen in the four months following the rate cuts [[7]](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/gst-rate-cuts-bring-relief-in-durables-not-essentials-nipfp-study-126031800527_1.html), while things like cars and air conditioners got clearly cheaper.

## The other side
**The case that GST raised prices:** Moving to a new tax system can let sellers round prices up, and some goods and services ended up taxed more heavily than before. The Kumar and Dash result (about +1.4 points on CPI, mostly non-food) is the strongest evidence for this.

**The government and RBI case:** GST replaced layers of taxes stacked on top of each other with one tax, and essentials were exempt or taxed lightly. Headline inflation fell, and the 2025 cuts showed that lowering GST rates lowers measured inflation.

**My analysis:** Both sides have a point. GST is a *system*, and whether it raises or lowers prices depends on the *rates* set for each item. Think of it like switching a highway from many toll booths to one: what you end up paying depends on the toll charged, not the switch itself. In 2017 some items seem to have gotten pricier, while in 2025 rate cuts made some things cheaper. I would give the same verdict for any country's sales-tax overhaul.

**Still unknown or disputed:** The studies use different methods and contradict each other, especially on food. Most are working papers, not final settled research.

## Verdict
**Not supported as stated. At most partly supported for specific non-food categories.** Confidence: **Medium.**

Inflation fell after GST, and the best evidence points to a modest one-off push on some items, not GST "causing inflation to go up" overall. What would change my mind: a peer-reviewed, official (for example RBI) breakdown showing a large, lasting GST-driven rise in headline CPI.

**Counter-Tweet:** Did GST cause inflation? Mostly no. India's CPI inflation fell from 4.5% (2016-17) to 3.6% (2017-18) and 3.4% (2018-19). Some studies find a push on non-food items, others find no effect. The 2025 GST cuts lowered CPI by about 0.25 points.

## What the evidence shows

2. The fee was more than ₹23 *before* tax.
3. The withdrawal failed and you got no cash. Reports say no charges apply in such cases [[4]](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rbi-hikes-atm-cash-withdrawal-fees-internet-banking-digital-transaction-125032501083_1.html).

### 12. Official data distrust
**Q:** Is the US government faking its CPI inflation numbers?
**Strength:** It cleanly separates 'faked' from 'poorly measured'. It backs this with documented, sourced examples: the shutdown rent carry-forward and its later reversal, the sample cuts with BLS's own simulation of their effect, and the IG findings limited to early releases. It covers critics who say the number is too low and those who say it is too high, and ends with a split verdict, confidence levels and clear falsification criteria.

**Weakness:** It never explains how BLS actually builds the CPI (basket, price collection, weights, the method behind owners' equivalent rent). It omits the classic substitution and quality-adjustment criticisms and cites no academic or independent cross-checks such as PriceStats or the Billion Prices Project. It overstates PCE as independent confirmation, even though PCE draws heavily on CPI source data. It leans on the weak inference that politically unflattering numbers cannot be faked. Citations 13–19 are missing from the source list, and several precise figures are hard to verify.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

**Short answer:** I found no evidence that anyone is faking the numbers. I did find real, documented problems with how well the CPI is measured. Those are two different things.

## What is claimed
The claim is that the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), which publishes the Consumer Price Index (CPI), is making up or bending inflation figures. Right now this suspicion comes from two opposite sides. Some people fear political pressure will push the numbers down. Others, including some who support the administration, say the official figure is too high.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The latest figure is bad news for the people in power.** In September 2026, the CPI-U rose 0.6 percent on a seasonally adjusted basis, after rising 0.4 percent in August [[1]](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm), and rose 3.7 percent over the 12 months ending September, up from 3.4 percent [[1]](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm). Gasoline alone rose 9.1 percent in September, more than half of the monthly increase. [[1]](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm) If someone were cooking the books for political gain, a rising number published just before a national election would be a strange result.

**2. A separate agency shows almost the same thing.** The Bureau of Economic Analysis reported headline PCE inflation of 3.6% and core PCE of 3.1% for September [[2]](https://stocktitan.net/articles/september-2026-pce-inflation-data). That is a different agency using a different method, and it lands close to the CPI.

**3. The worst known distortion pushed inflation *down*, and BLS disclosed it and fixed it.** During the 2025 shutdown, BLS could not collect October 2025 data [[3]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm). October rents were carried forward from April 2025, so the rent and owners' equivalent rent indexes showed no change for October. [[3]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/additional-resources/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cpi-faq.htm) Economists objected strongly. KPMG's Diane Swonk called it "a wacky number." [[4]](https://tipswatch.com/2025/12/21/whats-inside-the-foggy-november-inflation-report/) BLS later tested other methods, and its research suggested shelter inflation may have been understated by roughly 0.3% to 0.6% year over year during that stretch. [[5]](https://www.mortgagenewsdaily.com/news/05152026-cpi-shelter-shutdown) The error then reversed. Housing costs jumped 0.6% in April 2026 because BLS measured a full 12 months of rent increases instead of the usual 6. [[6]](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/last-shutdown-made-april-rent-105627401.html) That is a clumsy method applied in the open, not a hidden fake.

**4. Data quality really has gotten worse.** BLS stopped collecting CPI data in Buffalo, Lincoln and Provo, and cut about 15% of the sample in the other 72 areas. [[7]](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/labor-department-inspector-general-announces-194243241.html) BLS says a simulation going back to 2018 found this changed CPI estimates by less than 1/100th of a percentage point. [[7]](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/labor-department-inspector-general-announces-194243241.html) In 2025, the share of prices filled in by a lower-quality estimation method called "different-cell imputation" reached 36% [[8]](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/1180473408), according to a market news report. Also, more than a third of senior positions at the agency were estimated to be vacant at the start of 2026. [[9]](https://kfgo.com/2026/08/07/veteran-economist-insider-matsumoto-wins-senate-approval-as-bls-commissioner/)

**5. The political pressure is real too.** Trump fired Commissioner Erika McEntarfer, alleging without evidence that she had "faked" jobs numbers. [[10]](https://thehill.com/business/5498726-labor-department-watchdog-investigating-bls-data-collection/) His first replacement pick, E.J. Antoni, a BLS critic, was withdrawn after some Republicans opposed him. [[11]](https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2026-06-03/us-senate-to-hold-confirmation-hearing-for-bls-commissioner-nominee-next-week) The person now in charge, Brett Matsumoto, became Commissioner on August 11, 2026. [[12]](https://www.bls.gov/bls/senior_staff/matsumoto.htm) He joined BLS in 2015 as an inflation expert and was on leave at the White House Council of Economic Advisers when he was chosen. [[13]](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/brett-matsumoto-confirmed-as-bls-commissioner-after-predecessor-fired-93CH-4847326) Former commissioner Erica Groshen said he "doesn't have a history of publications that are political." [[14]](https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/veteran-economist-insider-matsumoto-wins-senate-approval-as-bls-commissioner-4847293) He also said changes to methods would be approved by career staff and explained publicly with supporting research. [[15]](https://www.c2er.org/2026/08/senate-confirms-brett-matsumoto-to-lead-bls/)

## The other side
**"The official number is too high."** Truflation, a private index, showed about 1.84–1.85% annual inflation in late June 2026 [[16]](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/truflation-reports-1-85-inflation-vs-4-2-from-bls-cathie-wood-highlights-discrepancy), while BLS reported 4.2% for May 2026. [[16]](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/truflation-reports-1-85-inflation-vs-4-2-from-bls-cathie-wood-highlights-discrepancy) The Heritage Foundation used this to argue that "real" inflation was only a third of the official figure. [[17]](https://www.heritage.org/markets-and-finance/commentary/less-1-percent-inflation-yes) But a big reason for the gap is how housing is counted. The CPI gives housing about a third of its weight, while Truflation gives it about a quarter. [[17]](https://www.heritage.org/markets-and-finance/commentary/less-1-percent-inflation-yes) Different design, different answer. That doesn't prove either one is faked.

**"Cuts and pressure are a back door to manipulation."** This is a fair worry. Fewer prices collected and more estimation mean more noise and less trust. But my searches found no audit, court or regulator showing deliberate falsification. The only recent CPI-related report I found from the Labor Department's Inspector General covered three incidents in 2024 where data was released improperly or too early [[18]](https://www.oig.dol.gov/public/Press%20Releases/OIG-Press-Release-063026.htm), not fabricated numbers. I could not find published results from the Inspector General's wider review of data-collection problems.

## Verdict
**"The government is faking CPI": Not supported. Confidence: high.** The current numbers are politically unflattering, a separate agency confirms them, and BLS published its own mistakes along with the fixes.

**"The CPI has become less reliable": Partly supported. Confidence: medium.** Smaller samples, more estimation and staff losses are documented, but BLS's own tests find small effects on the headline number.

**What would change my view:** an Inspector General or court finding of tampering, whistleblower evidence, or official CPI and PCE drifting apart from each other and from private measures for a long time without explanation.

One more point. The CPI measures an *average* basket. Over the past year, gasoline rose 34.2%, fuel oil 74.2% and airfares 22.4% [[19]](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/). If you drive a lot or heat with oil, your own inflation could be well above 3.7% without anyone lying.

## What you can do
- **Check the method yourself.** BLS publishes its methods, its corrections and its shutdown FAQ at bls.gov/cpi.
- **Look at more than one measure.** Compare CPI with the PCE index from the Bureau of Economic Analysis. If they agree, that's a good sign.
- **Work out your own inflation.** Compare what you spent this year on rent, fuel and groceries with last year. Your basket is what matters to your budget.
- **Remember the CPI sets real payments.** Social Security raises follow the CPI-W, and tax brackets follow the chained CPI. Quality problems hit your wallet, so it's worth telling your member of Congress you want BLS properly funded.

**Counter-Tweet:** No evidence US CPI is faked. It showed inflation rising to 3.7% (Sep 2026), hardly flattering to those in power, and the separate PCE index agrees. The real problems are thinner samples, more estimation and a shutdown data gap. Flawed ≠ fake.

### 13. Global consumer
**Q:** Are Nigerian banks allowed to charge me for every transfer I make?
**Strength:** It separates the capped bank fee from the ₦50 stamp-duty tax. The worked fee table matches the cited caps. It openly flags what it could not verify: the draft 2026 limits, the ~₦100 gap and the exemption scope. It also gives a concrete complaint route: bank first, the 30-day window, then cpd@cbn.gov.ng.

**Weakness:** The CBN Guide to Charges is cited only through secondary news sites, not the CBN document itself. The answer never says clearly which transfers carry no bank fee, such as same-bank transfers. That is the core of the 'every transfer' question. The banks' own case for charging, such as NIP/NIBSS processing costs, is absent from 'the other side'. Advice to combine transfers sits awkwardly with staying under ₦10,000 to avoid the stamp duty.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

Assuming you live in Nigeria and hold a normal personal account with a CBN-licensed bank or fintech:

**Short answer:** Yes, mostly. Banks are allowed to charge for transfers, but only up to limits set by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN). There is also a separate ₦50 government tax on larger transfers. Charges are legal. Charges above the cap, or charges with no explanation, are not.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The bank's transfer fee has a legal maximum.** The CBN's 2020 Guide to Charges, in effect since January 1, 2020, sets the rules for what financial institutions can deduct from customers. [[1]](https://abokiforex.app/show-news/1724998-cbn-releases-rule-bank-charges-atm-debit-cards-cash-transfers-affected) Under it, transfers below N5,000 attract a maximum of N10. Transactions between N5,001 and N50,000 attract N25. Transfers above N50,000 attract N50. [[1]](https://abokiforex.app/show-news/1724998-cbn-releases-rule-bank-charges-atm-debit-cards-cash-transfers-affected)

**2. Lower limits have been proposed, but it's unclear whether they apply yet.** On 21 April 2026 the CBN released a draft that would make transfers below ₦5,000 free, cap transfers from ₦5,000 to ₦50,000 at ₦10, and cap larger transfers at ₦50. [[2]](https://thegazette.ng/bank-charges-nigeria-2026-cbn-guide/) It listed May 1, 2026 as the start date. But as of early October, one Nigerian outlet reported that reports disagree on whether the draft is already in force, and we found no final version published by the CBN [[2]](https://thegazette.ng/bank-charges-nigeria-2026-cbn-guide/). I could not confirm a final CBN version either. To be safe, treat the 2020 limits as the upper limit and the draft as a possible lower one.

**3. The ₦50 "stamp duty" is a tax, not a bank fee.** Since January 1, 2026, banks have charged senders a N50 stamp duty on electronic transfers of N10,000 and above [[3]](https://www.thecable.ng/banks-to-start-charging-senders-n50-stamp-duty-on-transfers-above-n10k-from-january/) under the new tax law. This is the old Electronic Money Transfer Levy (EMTL) with a new name, and it used to be deducted from the Beneficiary/ Receiver. [[3]](https://www.thecable.ng/banks-to-start-charging-senders-n50-stamp-duty-on-transfers-above-n10k-from-january/) Banks say this charge is separate from regular bank transfer fees [[4]](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/12/31/banks-to-effect-%E2%82%A650-stamp-duty-on-transfers-above-%E2%82%A610k-from-january-1/). Two exemptions:
- Transactions below ₦10,000 remain fully exempt [[5]](https://www.okaynews.com/banks-to-charge-%E2%82%A650-stamp-duty-on-transfers-of-%E2%82%A610000-and-above-from-january/).
- Salary payments are exempt, and so are some transfers within the same bank. Reports differ on which ones: some say all same-bank transfers, while UBA's own notice says "Intra-bank self-transfers" [[3]](https://www.thecable.ng/banks-to-start-charging-senders-n50-stamp-duty-on-transfers-above-n10k-from-january/), meaning moving money between your own accounts. Ask your bank how it applies this.

## What a transfer to another bank costs you at most

| Amount sent | 2020 guide fee | Draft 2026 fee | Stamp duty | Total (2020 / draft) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₦3,000 | ₦10 | ₦0 | ₦0 | ₦10 / ₦0 |
| ₦8,000 | ₦25 | ₦10 | ₦0 | ₦25 / ₦10 |
| ₦50,000 | ₦25 | ₦10 | ₦50 | ₦75 / ₦60 |
| ₦200,000 | ₦50 | ₦50 | ₦50 | ₦100 / ₦100 |

These are maximum fees. Some reports put a ₦50,000 transfer at close to ₦100 in total charges [[6]](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/industry/1690611-opay-palmpay-moniepoint-alert-users-n50-stamp-duty-moves-senders-january-2026/), which suggests something else, possibly VAT on the fee, gets added. I could not verify what makes up that gap.

## The other side

The CBN says the draft aims to make digital payments cheaper. Reports say it hopes to incentivise the further adoption of electronic payment options by small businesses. [[7]](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/27/why-transfers-above-%E2%82%A610000-will-cost-%E2%82%A660/) The government says the stamp duty is a tax and not a bank profit. That's fair, but you still pay it. In 2026 the cost also moved from the receiver to you as the sender.

## Verdict

**Supported (High confidence):** Banks may charge on most transfers, within CBN limits, plus the ₦50 stamp duty on transfers of ₦10,000 and above. **Not enough evidence** on whether the cheaper 2026 limits are officially in force. A final CBN circular would settle that.

## What you can do

- **Check your alerts.** The CBN requires every alert or receipt for a charge to contain a description of the charge. [[8]](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/money/1651095-all-cbn-approved-bank-charges-savings-current-account/) Look for fees above the caps, a stamp duty on transfers under ₦10,000, or charges with no explanation. The CBN also says that charges not expressly captured in the guide are considered unauthorised [[9]](https://proshare.co/articles/cbn-releases-updated-guide-to-bank-charges-to-enhance-transparency-and-consumer-protection?menu=Economy&classification=Read&category=Monetary).
- **Complain to your bank first**, in writing, and keep a reference number. Banks must resolve complaints on excess charges and loans within thirty (30) days. [[10]](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Devfin/ldgcomplaint.asp)
- **If that fails, go to the CBN.** Email the Consumer Protection Department at cpd@cbn.gov.ng [[11]](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/money/1738296-how-dispute-unauthorised-bank-charges-recover-money-nigeria/). Attach your statement or alerts and proof that you complained to the bank first. Never send your PIN or password.
- **Combine transfers where you can.** Several small transfers each carry a fee, while staying under ₦10,000 avoids the stamp duty. Don't split a transfer just to dodge the tax, though.

**Counter-Tweet:** Nigerian banks can legally charge transfer fees, but only up to CBN limits (2020 guide: ₦10/₦25/₦50). The ₦50 stamp duty applies only to transfers of ₦10k and above. Overcharged? Complain to your bank, wait 30 days, then email cpd@cbn.gov.ng with proof.

### 14. Market claim
**Q:** A WhatsApp group promises 5% guaranteed monthly returns from crypto trading. Should I invest?
**Strength:** The compounding maths is correct (1.05^12 ≈ 1.80, so about 80% a year; 1.05^120 ≈ 349x over 10 years). It ties directly to NASAA red flags that match the scenario, gives concrete reporting steps (1930, cybercrime.gov.in, IC3) and warns about recovery scams. It also notes that the SEC cases are charges, not rulings.

**Weakness:** It never states that it cannot give personal investment advice. It opens with a flat 'No', which the rubric asks it to frame differently. A few assertions go beyond the cited sources: 'then blocked people when they tried to withdraw' carries no specific citation, and 'no licensed firm can lawfully promise guaranteed returns' is a broad legal claim with no source. The 'other side' rests on reasoning rather than evidence.

<details><summary>Full answer</summary>

**Short answer: No.** A "guaranteed" 5% a month from crypto trading has the main features of a scam. I can't check your particular group, but the promise alone gives you enough reason to walk away. I don't know which country you're in, and that only changes where you report it, not the answer.

## What is claimed
Strangers in a WhatsApp group say they can make you a fixed 5% every month, with no risk, by trading crypto.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The maths is impossible.** 5% a month, reinvested, works out like this:
- 1.05 multiplied by itself 12 times is about 1.80, so **around 80% a year**.
- Over 10 years, $1,000 would grow to about **$349,000**.

If anyone could really do that every month with no risk, they wouldn't need your savings. Banks would happily lend them money at a fraction of that rate. Crypto prices swing wildly, so nobody can honestly guarantee a fixed monthly return from trading it.

**2. Regulators warn about exactly this.** A US group of state securities regulators lists two red flags that match your situation: WhatsApp groups that offer investment advice from individuals you've never met in person, and guaranteed returns on investments. [[1]](https://www.nasaa.org/75050/informed-investor-advisory-crypto-scams-investment-education-ai-bots/) According to CryptoSlate, Investor.gov has issued alerts saying unsolicited investment clubs in messaging apps are a primary gateway for scams. [[2]](https://cryptoslate.com/sec-filings-reveal-the-14-million-trap-hiding-inside-exclusive-whatsapp-crypto-investment-clubs/)

**3. Real cases follow this script.** The US SEC has charged two offshore companies with defrauding over 2,000 retail investors of at least $15.3 million through fake AI-powered crypto trading platforms [[3]](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/criminal-law/sec-charges-whatsapp-driven-crypto-fraud-schemes-totaling-over-15-million). They allegedly used WhatsApp group chats, impersonated investment professionals, and fabricated regulatory filings [[3]](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/criminal-law/sec-charges-whatsapp-driven-crypto-fraud-schemes-totaling-over-15-million), then blocked people when they tried to withdraw. These are charges, not court rulings yet. In one of the schemes, the SEC alleges that the bots did not exist and that deposited funds were never used to generate returns. [[4]](https://news.bitcoin.com/regulation-and-legal/sec-charges-whatsapp-crypto-ai-scams-allegedly-taking-over-15-3m/)

**4. The losses are huge.** In the FBI's 2025 report, crypto investment fraud was the highest source of financial losses to Americans in 2025, with $7.2 billion in reported losses. [[5]](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/crypto-fraud-tops-fbis-annual-crime-report-americans-lose-billions-scams) The FBI says these scams typically start when scammers introduce victims to investment groups purporting to be knowledgeable industry insiders. [[5]](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/crypto-fraud-tops-fbis-annual-crime-report-americans-lose-billions-scams)

## The other side
The best case for the group is that crypto really can jump in a good month, and other members may be posting screenshots of profits. But:
- A good month is not a guaranteed month, every month.
- Screenshots are easy to fake, and "members" can be part of the scam.
- Scams often let you take out a small amount early to build trust. Then, when you invest more, withdrawals get blocked. A common trick is withdrawal fees and pressure to reinvest [[6]](https://coinpedia.org/news/sec-uncovers-14m-crypto-scam-that-lured-investors-through-whatsapp-groups/).

Think of it like a shop selling gold coins for half price, cash only, from a van. It's not a bargain. It's bait.

## Verdict
**Not supported: a guaranteed 5% a month from crypto trading isn't believable. Confidence: High.** It would take proof of a licence confirmed directly by your country's financial regulator, plus audited accounts, to change that. Even then, no licensed firm can lawfully promise guaranteed returns from trading.

## What you can do
- **Don't send money**, and don't install any app or "trading platform" they link to.
- **Check any firm directly with your country's financial regulator.** Don't trust licence numbers or certificates the group sends you.
- **Leave the group and report it** in WhatsApp.
- **If you've already paid:**
  - In India, call **1930**, the national helpline for financial cyber fraud. Calling quickly matters because it can initiate a hold on fraudulent transactions [[7]](https://policestationsindia.com/help/cybercrime-reporting). Then file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in and tell your bank.
  - In the US, report it to the FBI's IC3.
  - Elsewhere, contact your bank and the police cybercrime unit.
- **Watch out for a second scam.** Anyone who offers to get your money back for a fee is almost certainly another scammer. The FBI received more than 10,500 complaints about recovery scams last year, with an estimated $1.4 billion in losses. [[8]](https://www.asisonline.org/security-management-magazine/latest-news/today-in-security/2026/april/cryptocurrency-scams/)

If you tell me your country, I can point you to the exact regulator and helpline.

**Counter-Tweet:** "Guaranteed 5% a month" from crypto works out to about 80% a year, and no honest trader can promise that. WhatsApp investment groups promising guaranteed returns are a classic scam. Don't pay. Check with your financial regulator. Already paid in India? Call 1930.
